\subsection{Shared-site correction and the Sutherland relation}
\label{sec:shared-site-common-form}

For the scaling used below, the continuum source $\mathcal C$ can be expressed
in terms of the first coefficient of the normalized spatial Lax operator and
the corresponding order of the Sutherland relation.  Let $P$ denote the
permutation operator on two physical spin-$\tfrac12$ spaces, and assume
\begin{align}
\widehat L
&=\Id+\epsilon X+\epsilon^2Y+\mathcal O(\epsilon^3),
\nonumber\\
h
&=2P+\epsilon h^{(1)}+\mathcal O(\epsilon^2),
\nonumber\\
\partial_u\widehat L
&=\epsilon^2 Z+\mathcal O(\epsilon^3).
\label{eq:common-operator-expansions}
\end{align}
Here $X,Y,Z$ act on the auxiliary space and one physical site, while
$h^{(1)}$ acts on two neighbouring physical sites.  The labels $1,2$ below
specify the physical site on which a one-site operator acts; the auxiliary
space is shared and the order of auxiliary-space matrix products is retained.
For the scalings of Sections~\ref{sec:class5-model} and
\ref{sec:class6-model}, $Z$ is the coefficient obtained by differentiating
with respect to the quantum spectral parameter at fixed lattice coupling.

Define the order-$\epsilon^2$ Sutherland residual by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal B
:=[2P,X_2X_1+Y_2+Y_1]
+[h^{(1)},X_2+X_1]-Z_1+Z_2.
\label{eq:common-Sutherland-residual}
\end{equation}
For a lattice Lax operator satisfying the Sutherland relation at this order,
$\mathcal B=0$.  We also use the symmetric two-spin projector
$P_+:=(\Id+P)/2$ and impose
\begin{equation}
P_+h^{(1)}P_+=cP_+
\label{eq:common-triplet-condition}
\end{equation}
with a field-independent scalar $c$.

In Class 5 and Class 6, Eq.~\eqref{eq:common-triplet-condition} follows from
$\mathcal B=0$.  Expand
\begin{equation}
X=\mathsf X_0\otimes\Id_2+\mathsf X_i\otimes\sigma^i.
\label{eq:common-X-rank-expansion}
\end{equation}
If the three auxiliary-space matrices $\mathsf X_i$ are linearly independent,
the part of $\mathcal B=0$ that is even under exchange of the two physical
sites implies
\begin{equation}
[h^{(1)}_+,\sigma^i_1+\sigma^i_2]=0,
\qquad
h^{(1)}_+:=\frac12\left(h^{(1)}+Ph^{(1)}P\right),
\qquad i=1,2,3.
\label{eq:common-even-Sutherland}
\end{equation}
The tensor product of two spin-$\tfrac12$ representations contains one spin
triplet and one spin singlet.  An operator commuting with the three total-spin
generators is therefore scalar on each sector.  The exchange-odd part of
$h^{(1)}$ has vanishing triplet-to-triplet matrix elements, which gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:common-triplet-condition}.  For both Class 5 and Class 6 the
spin dependence has rank three for $\lambda\ne0$; one nonzero minor is
\begin{equation}
\det\frac{\partial(U_{12},U_{21},U_{11})}
{\partial(S^1,S^2,S^3)}
=\frac{\ii}{32\lambda^3}.
\label{eq:common-model-rank-minor}
\end{equation}
For a rank-deficient $X$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:common-triplet-condition} need not
follow from the Sutherland relation, so it remains an explicit assumption in
the general formula.

At a fixed continuum spin $\boldsymbol S$, define
\begin{equation}
U:=X^\downarrow,
\qquad
\Xi:=(X^2)^\downarrow-U^2,
\label{eq:common-Xi-definition}
\end{equation}
where the operator square is formed before taking the lower symbol.  The
quantity $\Xi$ is the finite-spin symbol correction associated with this
operator product, as described equivalently by coherent-state star-product
calculus \cite{KoczorZeierGlaser2019}.  The local lattice time equation fixes
how this correction enters the long-wavelength expansion.  Expanding the
finite-lattice time operator gives
\begin{align}
A^{(1)}&=-\ii[2P,X_2],
\nonumber\\
A^{(2)}&=\ii X_2[2P,X_2]-\ii[2P,Y_2]
-\ii[h^{(1)},X_2]-\ii Z_2.
\label{eq:common-time-coefficients}
\end{align}
We suppress the model label $m$ on the coefficient functions
$\mathcal D^{(\pm)}_{m,2}$ and $\mathcal D^{(\pm)}_{m,3}$ introduced in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:overlap-plus-coefficients} and
\eqref{eq:overlap-minus-coefficients}.  We also write $\langle Q\rangle$ for
the lower symbol of a two-physical-site operator $Q$ evaluated with the same
spin $\boldsymbol S$ on both sites, leaving its auxiliary-space indices
uncontracted.  The analytic derivation of the next three identities is given
in Appendix~\ref{app:shared-site-derivation}.

At coincident spins, the order-$\epsilon^2$ coefficients satisfy
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D^{(+)}_2(\boldsymbol S,\boldsymbol S)
=\mathcal D^{(-)}_2(\boldsymbol S,\boldsymbol S)
=2\ii\Xi.
\label{eq:common-quadratic-overlap}
\end{equation}
Their first spatial Taylor coefficients give
\begin{equation}
\partial_x S^i
\left.
\left(
\frac{\partial\mathcal D^{(+)}_2}{\partial S_R^i}
+\frac{\partial\mathcal D^{(-)}_2}{\partial S_L^i}
\right)\right|_{\boldsymbol S_L=\boldsymbol S_R=\boldsymbol S}
=2\ii\partial_x\Xi.
\label{eq:common-quadratic-Taylor}
\end{equation}
The coincident order-$\epsilon^3$ coefficients obey
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D^{(+)}_3(\boldsymbol S,\boldsymbol S)
-\mathcal D^{(-)}_3(\boldsymbol S,\boldsymbol S)
=2\ii[\Xi,U]-\ii\langle X_1\mathcal B\rangle.
\label{eq:common-cubic-overlap}
\end{equation}
The explicit $Y$ terms cancel after the contributions in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:common-time-coefficients} are combined.  Using
Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlap-source-taylor},
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C
=2\ii\bigl(\partial_x\Xi+[\Xi,U]\bigr)
-\ii\langle X_1\mathcal B\rangle.
\label{eq:common-source-with-residual}
\end{equation}
For $\mathcal B=0$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C
=2\ii\bigl(\partial_x\Xi+[\Xi,U]\bigr),
\label{eq:common-source-general}
\end{equation}
which is absorbed by
\begin{equation}
\Delta V=2\ii\Xi+f(\lambda)\Id,
\label{eq:common-DeltaV-general}
\end{equation}
where $f(\lambda)$ is independent of the spin and spacetime coordinates.

For spin $\tfrac12$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:common-X-rank-expansion} gives
$U=\mathsf X_0+\mathsf X_iS^i$ and
\begin{equation}
\Xi
=\mathsf X_i\mathsf X_j
\left(\delta_{ij}-S^iS^j+\ii\varepsilon_{ijk}S^k\right).
\label{eq:common-Xi-from-U}
\end{equation}
In Class 6, Eq.~\eqref{eq:class6-L-first-square} gives an equivalent
representation of $\Xi$ in terms of $L_6^{(2)}$.

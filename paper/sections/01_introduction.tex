\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Landau--Lifshitz model relates the long-wavelength dynamics of
quantum spin chains to classical field theory.  It also appears in
limits of string sigma models: the coherent-state continuum action
of the Heisenberg chain agrees with an action obtained for strings
carrying large angular momentum \cite{Kruczenski2004}.  Integrable
deformations extend this relation to anisotropic Landau--Lifshitz
models, whose Lax pairs and conserved-charge algebras have been studied
in limits of deformed string backgrounds \cite{KameyamaYoshida2014}.

The Class 5 and Class 6 models provide two non-Hermitian deformations
of the spin-$\tfrac12$ Heisenberg XXX chain.  They arose in the
classification of nearest-neighbour integrable chains in
Ref.~\cite{deLeeuwPribytokRyan2019} and have transfer matrices with
nontrivial Jordan blocks.  De Leeuw, Fontanella and Nieto Garc\'ia
derived their deformed Landau--Lifshitz continuum models and
constructed higher conserved charges using a boost functional
\cite{deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026}.  For Class 5, they also
identified the quantum deformation as a Jordanian Drinfeld twist.

Coherent-state continuum limits that retain classical Lax and
$r$-matrix structures were formulated in
Ref.~\cite{AvanDoikouSfetsos2010}.  Quantum time Lax operators can be
constructed from the lattice monodromy \cite{DoikouFindlay2020}, and
continuum Hamiltonians, time Lax matrices and boundary conditions were
developed further in Ref.~\cite{DoikouKaraiskos2011}.  The calculation
below concerns the long-wavelength limit of the local finite-lattice
zero-curvature equation.

The final classical structures are related to known models.  Class 6 is the
eleven-vertex model \cite{CorcoranDeLeeuw2024}; its Landau--Lifshitz hierarchy
follows from the eleven-vertex construction of Levin, Olshanetsky and Zotov
\cite{LevinOlshanetskyZotov2014}, with explicit rational Lax matrices given in
Ref.~\cite{AtalikovZotov2021}.  For Class 5,
Ref.~\cite{deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026} constructs a spatial Lax matrix
but does not give a compatible time component in those variables.  The pair
obtained here is locally related, after a complex field redefinition and an
auxiliary-space gauge transformation, to the null-like warped $SL(2)$
Landau--Lifshitz pair of Ref.~\cite{KameyamaYoshida2014}.  These known pairs
provide independent checks of the continuum time matrices obtained from the
lattice calculation.

We take the continuum limit at fixed spin $\tfrac12$ and use coherent-state
lower symbols, with auxiliary-space indices retained.  The lattice
zero-curvature equation contains products whose factors share a physical
site.  At that site the lower symbol of a product generally differs from the
product of the separate symbols, as in standard coherent-state symbol
calculus \cite{Perelomov1986,Schlichenmaier2010}.  Smooth variation of the
spin labels controls the spatial expansion, while the common site retains the
fixed spin-$\tfrac12$ operator algebra.

For both chains, the connected contributions start at order $\epsilon^2$ and
cancel at coincident spins.  Their first spatial Taylor coefficients and the
coincident order-$\epsilon^3$ products remain at the same order as the
continuum time evolution.  If $X$ is the first coefficient of the normalized
spatial lattice operator, define
\begin{equation}
\Xi=(X^2)^\downarrow-(X^\downarrow)^2.
\end{equation}
Under the conditions stated in Section~\ref{sec:shared-site-common-form}, the
Sutherland relation gives
$2\ii(\partial_x\Xi+[\Xi,U])$ for the surviving source, which can be absorbed
into the time matrix.

For Class 5 the source is a total spatial derivative.  For Class 6 its
model-specific representation contains a nonzero commutator, visible already
for a spatially uniform spin configuration.  In both cases the corrected
zero-curvature equations are equivalent off shell to the independently
derived Hamiltonian equations, with explicit inverse relations.  The Class 5
time matrix also agrees with an independent classical $r$-matrix construction
up to a field-independent scalar matrix.  The Class 6 pair agrees, after the
change of conventions given in Section~\ref{sec:class6-eleven-vertex-comparison},
with the rational eleven-vertex Landau--Lifshitz pair.  In the undeformed XXX
limit, omitting the shared-site contribution gives the wrong coefficient in
the standard time matrix.

We also use numerical coefficient searches to identify candidate Lax pairs,
following the optimization approach of Krippendorf, L\"ust and Syvaeri
\cite{KrippendorfLustSyvaeri2021}.  The input consists of sampled local spin
values and derivatives, and the coefficients of finite ans\"atze are fitted
at fixed deformation and spectral parameters by minimizing off-shell
curvature residuals.  The tensor structures use the null direction in Class 5
and the nilpotent anisotropy in Class 6.  In Class 5, eight additional
candidate coefficients are suppressed; in Class 6, the fitted relations give
a finite matrix-square-root expression.  Appendix~\ref{sec:ml-search} gives
the ans\"atze, loss functions, numerical settings and analytic checks.

Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes the lattice conventions and derives the common
shared-site correction.  Sections~\ref{sec:class5-model} and
\ref{sec:class6-model} treat the two chains.  Their model-dependent terms and
the isotropic limit are compared in Section~\ref{sec:comparison}, followed by
Section~\ref{sec:summary-discussion}.

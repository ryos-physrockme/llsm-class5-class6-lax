\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Landau--Lifshitz model relates the long-wavelength dynamics of
quantum spin chains to classical field theory.  It also appears in
limits of string sigma models: the coherent-state continuum action
of the Heisenberg chain agrees with an action obtained for strings
carrying large angular momentum \cite{Kruczenski2004}.  Integrable
deformations extend this relation to anisotropic Landau--Lifshitz
models.  Their classical Lax pairs and conserved-charge algebras have
been studied in limits of deformed string backgrounds
\cite{KameyamaYoshida2014}.  These connections motivate an explicit
account of how the Hamiltonian and Lax structures pass from the
quantum chain to the continuum theory.

The Class 5 and Class 6 models provide two non-Hermitian deformations
of the spin-$\tfrac12$ Heisenberg XXX chain.  They arose in the
classification of nearest-neighbour integrable chains in
Ref.~\cite{deLeeuwPribytokRyan2019} and have transfer matrices with
nontrivial Jordan blocks.  De Leeuw, Fontanella and Nieto Garc\'ia
derived their deformed Landau--Lifshitz continuum models and
constructed higher conserved charges using a boost functional
\cite{deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026}.  For Class 5, they also
identified the quantum deformation as a Jordanian Drinfeld twist.
The coexistence of this algebraic structure with a non-Hermitian
Hamiltonian makes these chains useful examples for studying the
relation between quantum and classical integrability.

Several ingredients of this correspondence are already available in
general form.  Avan, Doikou and Sfetsos formulated coherent-state
continuum limits that retain classical Lax and $r$-matrix structures
\cite{AvanDoikouSfetsos2010}.  Quantum time Lax operators and their
hierarchy can be constructed from the lattice monodromy
\cite{DoikouFindlay2020}, while continuum Hamiltonians, time Lax
matrices and boundary conditions were developed further in
Ref.~\cite{DoikouKaraiskos2011}.  These results provide the spatial
continuum construction and the finite-lattice time evolution separately.
The issue considered here is the local passage between them: the
long-wavelength limit of the finite-lattice zero-curvature equation itself.

For Class 6, the quantum model is identified with the eleven-vertex model in
Ref.~\cite{CorcoranDeLeeuw2024}, and a corresponding Landau--Lifshitz
hierarchy is known from the eleven-vertex construction of Levin,
Olshanetsky and Zotov \cite{LevinOlshanetskyZotov2014}.  Explicit rational
Landau--Lifshitz Lax matrices in compatible conventions are also available
in Ref.~\cite{AtalikovZotov2021}.  We therefore do not seek a new Class 6
hierarchy.  For Class 5, Ref.~\cite{deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026}
constructs a spatial Lax matrix but leaves a compatible time component open.
These two models allow the same finite-lattice-to-continuum construction to
be tested both against an open time-component problem and against an
independently known classical pair.

We perform the limit at fixed spin $\tfrac12$, using the coherent-state
lower symbol: the matrix element of an operator in a product of spin
coherent states, with auxiliary-space indices retained.  The lattice
zero-curvature equation contains products whose factors share a physical
site.  At that site the lower symbol of a product generally differs from
the product of the separate symbols, as in standard coherent-state symbol
calculus \cite{Perelomov1986,Schlichenmaier2010}.  Smooth variation of the
spin labels controls the long-wavelength expansion, while a common physical
site retains the fixed spin-$\tfrac12$ operator algebra.

The point relevant to the time equation is the power counting of these
products.  In the scaling realized by both chains, the two connected
contributions start at order $\epsilon^2$ and cancel at coincident spins.
Their first spatial Taylor coefficients and the coincident order-$\epsilon^3$
products remain at the same order as the continuum time evolution.  Let $X$
denote the first coefficient of the normalized spatial lattice operator.  We
show under explicit spin-$\tfrac12$ assumptions that the surviving terms are
controlled by the second-moment difference
\begin{equation}
\Xi=(X^2)^\downarrow-(X^\downarrow)^2.
\end{equation}
When the corresponding order of the Sutherland relation holds, the source has
the local form $2\ii(\partial_x\Xi+[\Xi,U])$ and is absorbed into the time
matrix.  The two model calculations independently verify this common
identity.

For Class 5 the source reduces to a total spatial derivative.  For Class 6
its model-specific representation also contains a nonzero commutator, which
can be isolated on a spatially uniform spin configuration.  In both cases
we derive the Hamiltonian equations independently and establish their
off-shell equivalence to the corrected zero-curvature equations with
explicit inverse relations.  The Class 5 time matrix agrees with an
independent classical $r$-matrix construction up to a field-independent
scalar matrix.  The Class 6 pair agrees, after an explicit change of
spectral, coupling and time conventions, with the known rational
eleven-vertex Landau--Lifshitz pair.  The undeformed XXX limit provides a
further control: omitting the shared-site contribution gives the wrong
coefficient in the standard time matrix even in that limit.

We also use numerical coefficient searches to identify candidate Lax pairs,
following the optimization approach of
Krippendorf, L\"ust and Syvaeri \cite{KrippendorfLustSyvaeri2021}.
The input consists of sampled local spin values and derivatives, and the
coefficients of finite ans\"atze are fitted at fixed deformation and spectral
parameters by minimizing off-shell curvature residuals.  The tensor
structures are specified using the null direction in Class 5 and the
nilpotent anisotropy in Class 6.  In Class 5, the coefficients of eight
additional candidate terms were suppressed; in Class 6, the recovered
relations revealed a finite matrix-square-root expression.
Appendix~\ref{sec:ml-search} gives the ans\"atze, loss functions, numerical
settings and analytic verification.

Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes the lattice conventions, formulates the
continuum problem and derives the common shared-site correction.
Sections~\ref{sec:class5-model} and \ref{sec:class6-model} apply the
construction to the two chains and provide independent checks against their
continuum dynamics and classical Lax structures.  Section~\ref{sec:comparison}
compares the model-dependent realizations and the isotropic limit.  We
summarize the results and discuss their scope and possible extensions in
Section~\ref{sec:summary-discussion}.

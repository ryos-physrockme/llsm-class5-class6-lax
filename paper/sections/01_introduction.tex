\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Landau--Lifshitz models arise as long-wavelength descriptions of quantum
spin chains and provide a standard setting in which quantum and classical
integrability can be compared.  For the Heisenberg chain, the coherent-state
continuum limit also reproduces the effective action obtained from strings
with large angular momentum \cite{Kruczenski2004}.  Integrable deformations
lead to anisotropic Landau--Lifshitz models and connect spin-chain
deformations with deformed string sigma models
\cite{KameyamaYoshida2014}.

The continuum limit of an integrable lattice model involves more than its
Hamiltonian.  Coherent-state limits of lattice monodromies and spatial Lax
operators were developed systematically in
Ref.~\cite{AvanDoikouSfetsos2010}, while classical continuum Hamiltonians,
time Lax matrices and boundary terms were studied in
Ref.~\cite{DoikouKaraiskos2011}.  On the quantum lattice, the time component
can be generated from the monodromy and the local Hamiltonians
\cite{DoikouFindlay2020}.  In the global lattice sums considered in
Ref.~\cite{AvanDoikouSfetsos2010}, coincident site labels reduce the number of
independent sums and are subleading in the leading continuum contribution.
The local zero-curvature equation considered here has a different power
counting: its two shared-site products have leading contributions that cancel,
while the first spatial Taylor terms and the next operator-product terms enter
at the order of the continuum time derivative.

The Class 5 and Class 6 chains considered here are two non-Hermitian
spin-$\tfrac12$ deformations of the XXX chain that appear in the
classification of regular nearest-neighbour integrable Hamiltonians
\cite{deLeeuwPribytokRyan2019}.  Their transfer matrices can have nontrivial
Jordan blocks.  De Leeuw, Fontanella and Nieto Garc\'ia derived the associated
Landau--Lifshitz continuum models and higher conserved charges
\cite{deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026}.  In Class 5 the deformation is
related to a Jordanian Drinfeld twist.  The same work constructed a classical
spatial Lax matrix from the Class 5 quantum Lax operator and established its
quadratic Poisson algebra; a compatible time Lax matrix for that construction
was left open.  We use the same Class 5 quantum $R$-matrix family in the fundamental
representation.  Its normalized spin-$\tfrac12$ lower symbol has the spatial
matrix $U_5$ of Section~\ref{sec:class5-spatial-lax} as the leading
long-wavelength coefficient, and the finite-lattice time operator determines
the corresponding continuum time matrix below.

The classical structures reached by these deformations are related to known
integrable models.  The null-like warped $SL(2)$ Landau--Lifshitz model of
Kameyama and Yoshida is associated with a Jordanian deformation and comes
with a classical Lax representation \cite{KameyamaYoshida2014}.  Class 6 is
the eleven-vertex model at the quantum level
\cite{CorcoranDeLeeuw2024}; the corresponding rational Landau--Lifshitz
hierarchy is known \cite{LevinOlshanetskyZotov2014,AtalikovZotov2021}.
These results provide independent classical reference points for the two
continuum limits.

For the Class 5 and Class 6 chains, the question addressed here is the local
quantum-to-classical passage for the time component.  At fixed spin
$\tfrac12$, smoothness suppresses differences between neighbouring
coherent-state labels, while products on a common site retain the finite-spin
operator algebra.  The resulting nonmultiplicativity of lower symbols is
standard in coherent-state symbol calculus
\cite{Perelomov1986,Schlichenmaier2010}; finite-spin star products provide an
explicit symbol calculus for such operator products
\cite{KoczorZeierGlaser2019}.  The calculation below tracks these shared-site
products through the order relevant to the local time-evolution equation and
uses the Sutherland relation to combine the surviving terms into a local
source.  The undeformed XXX chain provides a useful control: replacing the
shared-site products by products of separate lower symbols gives an incorrect
coefficient in the standard time matrix.

In this paper we take the finite-lattice time Lax operators of the Class 5
and Class 6 chains to the long-wavelength limit while retaining these
shared-site products.  A common spin-$\tfrac12$ expression for the resulting
source follows from the corresponding order of the Sutherland relation.  In
Class 5 the source reduces to a total spatial derivative, whereas in Class 6
it contains a nonzero commutator contribution.  For both models the corrected
zero-curvature equations are equivalent off shell to the Hamiltonian
equations of motion.  The Class 5 result is compared with both an independent
classical $r$-matrix construction and the null-like warped $SL(2)$ model; the
Class 6 result is matched to the rational eleven-vertex Landau--Lifshitz
pair.

Numerical coefficient searches were used during the construction of the two
pairs, following the optimization approach of
Ref.~\cite{KrippendorfLustSyvaeri2021}.  The final statements are established
analytically; the ans\"atze, losses and numerical checks are collected in
Appendix~\ref{sec:ml-search}.

Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes the lattice and continuum conventions and
derives the shared-site contribution.  Sections~\ref{sec:class5-model} and
\ref{sec:class6-model} treat Class 5 and Class 6, respectively.
Section~\ref{sec:comparison} compares their continuum corrections and the XXX
limit.  Section~\ref{sec:summary-discussion} summarizes the results and their
scope.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Landau--Lifshitz models arise as long-wavelength limits of quantum spin
chains and provide a standard setting for comparing quantum and classical
integrability.  For the Heisenberg chain, the coherent-state limit also
reproduces the effective action of strings with large angular momentum \cite{Kruczenski2004}.  Integrable deformations
lead to anisotropic Landau--Lifshitz models and connect spin-chain
deformations with deformed string sigma models
\cite{KameyamaYoshida2014}.

The continuum limit of an integrable lattice model involves more than its
Hamiltonian.  Coherent-state limits of lattice monodromies and spatial Lax
operators were developed systematically in
Ref.~\cite{AvanDoikouSfetsos2010}, while continuum Hamiltonians, time Lax
matrices and boundary terms were studied in
Ref.~\cite{DoikouKaraiskos2011}.  On the quantum lattice, the time component
can be generated from the monodromy and local Hamiltonians
\cite{DoikouFindlay2020}.  In global lattice sums, coincident site labels can
be subleading because they reduce the number of independent sums
\cite{AvanDoikouSfetsos2010}.  A local lattice zero-curvature equation has a
different structure: its neighbouring factors can overlap on a physical site,
so the corresponding operator products must be treated before taking a
coherent-state continuum limit.

The Class 5 and Class 6 chains are two non-Hermitian spin-$\tfrac12$
deformations of the Heisenberg XXX chain that appear in the classification of
regular nearest-neighbour integrable Hamiltonians
\cite{deLeeuwPribytokRyan2019}.  Their transfer matrices can have nontrivial
Jordan blocks.  The associated Landau--Lifshitz continuum models and higher
conserved charges were derived in
Ref.~\cite{deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026}.  In Class 5 the deformation is
related to a Jordanian Drinfeld twist.  The same work constructed a classical
spatial Lax matrix from the Class 5 quantum Lax operator and established its
quadratic Poisson algebra, while a compatible time component within that
construction was left open.

A null-like warped $SL(2)$ Landau--Lifshitz model with a classical Lax
representation was studied in
Ref.~\cite{KameyamaYoshida2014}.  Class 6 is the eleven-vertex model at the
quantum level \cite{CorcoranDeLeeuw2024}, and the corresponding rational
Landau--Lifshitz hierarchy is known
\cite{LevinOlshanetskyZotov2014,AtalikovZotov2021}.  These representations provide reference points for deriving the continuum
time component directly from the quantum lattice Lax structure.

At fixed spin $\tfrac12$, the quantum-to-classical passage requires care
with operator products that share a physical site.  Smoothness suppresses
differences between neighbouring coherent-state labels.  Products acting on a
common site retain the finite-spin operator algebra.  This nonmultiplicativity is standard
in coherent-state symbol calculus
\cite{Perelomov1986,Schlichenmaier2010}, and finite-spin star products provide
an explicit description of such products
\cite{KoczorZeierGlaser2019}.  The relevant question for the local lattice
zero-curvature equation is therefore which shared-site terms survive the
long-wavelength expansion and whether they can be incorporated into a local
continuum Lax pair.

In this paper we derive the continuum Lax correspondence for the Class 5 and
Class 6 chains directly from their finite-lattice Lax equations.  The leading
shared-site contributions cancel, while the first spatial Taylor terms and the
next operator-product terms survive at the order of the continuum time
evolution.  Using the corresponding order of the Sutherland relation, these
terms combine into a local correction determined by the second moment of the
leading spatial Lax coefficient.  In Class 5 the resulting source is a total
spatial derivative, whereas in Class 6 it contains a nonzero commutator.  For
both models the corrected zero-curvature equations are equivalent off shell to
the independently derived Hamiltonian equations.  The Class 5 result agrees,
up to the appropriate local transformations, with both an independent
classical $r$-matrix construction and the null-like warped $SL(2)$
Landau--Lifshitz representation, while the Class 6 result maps to the rational
eleven-vertex pair.  The undeformed XXX limit provides a further check:
neglecting the shared-site contribution gives an incorrect coefficient in the
standard time matrix.

Numerical coefficient searches assisted the construction of the two pairs,
following Ref.~\cite{KrippendorfLustSyvaeri2021}.  The final statements are
analytic; the numerical ans\"atze, losses and checks are included in the
appendix.

Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes the lattice and continuum conventions and
derives the shared-site contribution.  Sections~\ref{sec:class5-model} and
\ref{sec:class6-model} treat Class 5 and Class 6, respectively.
Section~\ref{sec:comparison} compares their continuum corrections and the XXX
limit.  Section~\ref{sec:summary-discussion} summarizes the results and their
scope.

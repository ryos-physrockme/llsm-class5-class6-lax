\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Landau--Lifshitz model relates the long-wavelength dynamics of
quantum spin chains to classical field theory.  It also appears in
limits of string sigma models: the coherent-state continuum action
of the Heisenberg chain agrees with an action obtained for strings
carrying large angular momentum \cite{Kruczenski2004}.  Integrable
deformations extend this relation to anisotropic Landau--Lifshitz
models.  Their classical Lax pairs and conserved-charge algebras have
been studied in limits of deformed string backgrounds
\cite{KameyamaYoshida2014}.  These connections motivate an explicit
account of how the Hamiltonian and Lax structures pass from the
quantum chain to the continuum theory.

The Class 5 and Class 6 models provide two non-Hermitian deformations
of the spin-$\tfrac12$ Heisenberg XXX chain.  They arose in the
classification of nearest-neighbour integrable chains in
Ref.~\cite{deLeeuwPribytokRyan2019} and have transfer matrices with
nontrivial Jordan blocks.  De Leeuw, Fontanella and Nieto Garc\'ia
derived their deformed Landau--Lifshitz continuum models and
constructed higher conserved charges using a boost functional
\cite{deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026}.  For Class 5, they also
identified the quantum deformation as a Jordanian Drinfeld twist.
The coexistence of this algebraic structure with a non-Hermitian
Hamiltonian makes these chains useful examples for studying the
relation between quantum and classical integrability.

Several classical constructions are available for this comparison.
Avan, Doikou and Sfetsos formulated continuum limits that retain the
classical Lax and $r$-matrix structures \cite{AvanDoikouSfetsos2010}.
The relation between continuum Hamiltonians, time Lax matrices and
boundary conditions was developed further in
Ref.~\cite{DoikouKaraiskos2011}.  For Class 6, the quantum model is
identified with the eleven-vertex model in
Ref.~\cite{CorcoranDeLeeuw2024}, and its Landau--Lifshitz Lax
formulation is already contained in the eleven-vertex construction
of Levin, Olshanetsky and Zotov \cite{LevinOlshanetskyZotov2014}.

The question addressed here is how the quantum lattice \emph{time}
Lax operator produces the continuum time matrix for the Hamiltonian
flow.  In Section~2 of Ref.~\cite{deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026},
the authors discuss a spatial Lax matrix for Class 5 and leave the
construction of a compatible time component open.  Taking the time
operator directly to the continuum identifies the quantum terms
that enter this component and permits a comparison with the
classical $r$-matrix construction.

We perform this limit at fixed spin $\tfrac12$, using the
coherent-state lower symbol: the matrix element of an operator in
a product of spin coherent states, with auxiliary-space indices
retained.  The lattice zero-curvature equation contains operator
products whose factors share a physical site.  At that site, the
lower symbol of a product generally differs from the product of
the separate symbols, as in standard coherent-state symbol calculus
\cite{Perelomov1986,Schlichenmaier2010}.  Smooth variation of the spin
labels between neighbouring sites controls the long-wavelength
expansion, while products on a common site retain the fixed
spin-$\tfrac12$ operator algebra.  Their contribution must therefore
be evaluated at the order of the continuum time evolution.

For both chains, we compute this surviving contribution and absorb
it into a local correction of the direct time symbol.  In Class 5
it is a total spatial derivative; Class 6 also has a commutator
contribution involving the second spatial-operator coefficient.
We derive the Hamiltonian equations independently and establish
their equivalence to the corrected zero-curvature equations by
identities that hold before imposing the equations of motion.
Explicit inverse relations recover every component of the equations
from the curvature.  The undeformed XXX limit and the independent
classical $r$-matrix construction for Class 5 provide further checks.

We also determine Lax pairs through numerical coefficient searches,
following the optimization approach of
Krippendorf, L\"ust and Syvaeri \cite{KrippendorfLustSyvaeri2021}.
The input consists of sampled local spin values and derivatives, and the
coefficients of finite ans\"atze are fitted by minimizing off-shell
curvature residuals.  The tensor structures are specified using the null
direction in Class 5 and the nilpotent anisotropy in Class 6.
In Class 5, the coefficients of eight additional candidate terms were
suppressed; in Class 6, the recovered relations revealed a finite
matrix-square-root expression.  Appendix~\ref{sec:ml-search} gives the
ans\"atze, loss functions, numerical settings and analytic verification.

Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes the lattice conventions and formulates the
continuum problem.  Sections~\ref{sec:class5-model} and
\ref{sec:class6-model} carry out the two model calculations.
Section~\ref{sec:comparison} compares the surviving corrections and
their dependence on the lattice operators.  We summarize the results
and discuss their scope and possible extensions in
Section~\ref{sec:summary-discussion}.

\section{Machine-learning searches for the Lax pairs}
\label{sec:ml-search}

We determine coefficients in finite Lax ans\"atze by minimizing an
off-shell residual, following the optimization approach of
Ref.~\cite{KrippendorfLustSyvaeri2021}.  The input data are local values
of the spin and its derivatives,
$(\boldsymbol S,\partial_x\boldsymbol S,
\partial_x^2\boldsymbol S,\partial_t\boldsymbol S)$.
These are sampled subject to the unit-spin constraint and its differentiated
identities, with the time derivative independent of the equations of motion.
The sampling distributions are specified in Section~\ref{sec:ml-numerics}.

For each model $m\in\{5,6\}$, a run holds the deformation parameter
$\alpha_m$ and spectral parameter $\lambda$ fixed.  The equations of motion
and the tensor structures allowed in the Lax ansatz are also supplied.
The trainable parameters are the coefficients of those structures: thirteen
real coefficients for Class 5 and eight for Class 6, each shared by all
samples in a run.  They are adjusted so that the curvature agrees with the
equation-of-motion residual multiplied by the coefficient map determined
from the spatial ansatz.  This yields a candidate Lax pair at the chosen
$(\alpha_m,\lambda)$.  Training requires no target values of a Lax pair.
The numerical runs use real $\alpha_m$ and $\lambda\in\ii\mathbb R_{>0}$,
for which the coefficient relations below are real.  Their analytic verification applies
to complex $\lambda\ne0$.

To write the ans\"atze, represent traceless matrices by three-component
vectors.  With the Pauli matrices of Section~\ref{sec:setup}, define
\begin{equation}
\iota(\boldsymbol v):=-\frac{\ii}{2}v^i\sigma^i,
\qquad
[\iota(\boldsymbol v),\iota(\boldsymbol w)]
=\iota(\boldsymbol v\times\boldsymbol w).
\label{eq:ml-iota}
\end{equation}
Write the candidate matrices as
$U_m^{\rm fit}=\mu_m\Id_2+\iota(\boldsymbol u_m)$ and
$V_m^{\rm fit}=\nu_m\Id_2+\iota(\boldsymbol v_m)$.
Here $\boldsymbol u_m,\boldsymbol v_m$ contain the trainable coefficients,
while the scalar parts $\mu_m,\nu_m$ are prescribed functions, specified
below.  The superscript distinguishes the fitted candidate from the
analytic matrices in the main text.
The vector part of its curvature is
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\mathcal F}^{(m)}
:=\partial_t\boldsymbol u_m-\partial_x\boldsymbol v_m
  +\boldsymbol u_m\times\boldsymbol v_m.
\label{eq:ml-vector-curvature}
\end{equation}
The vectors $\boldsymbol E^{(5)}$ and $\boldsymbol E^{(6)}$ denote
$\partial_t\boldsymbol S$ minus the right-hand side of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:class5-vector-eom} and \eqref{eq:class6-vector-eom},
respectively.  They are computed from the input data at each sampled point.
All spin contractions and cross products are complex bilinear.
In the losses, $\|\boldsymbol v\|^2=\sum_i|v^i|^2$ is the positive
Euclidean norm squared, and angle brackets denote the arithmetic mean
over the training samples.

\subsection{Class 5}
\label{sec:ml-class5-paper}

The constant matrix $M_5$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:class5-M-definition} can be
written as $M_5\boldsymbol v=\boldsymbol m_5\times\boldsymbol v$, where
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol m_5=(\ii,-1,0)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad \boldsymbol m_5^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol m_5=0,
\qquad M_5^2=\boldsymbol m_5\boldsymbol m_5^{\mathsf T}.
\label{eq:ml-null-vector}
\end{equation}
The ansatz uses the spatial differential operator
$\mathcal D_x\boldsymbol v:=\partial_x\boldsymbol v
+(\alpha_5/2)M_5\boldsymbol v$ and the constant auxiliary-basis rotation
\begin{equation}
O_5(\lambda):=\exp(\alpha_5\lambda M_5)
=\Id_3+\alpha_5\lambda M_5
 +\frac{\alpha_5^2\lambda^2}{2}M_5^2,
\qquad O_5^{\mathsf T}O_5=\Id_3,
\qquad\det O_5=1.
\label{eq:ml-class5-rotation}
\end{equation}
Both $\mathcal D_x$ and $O_5$ are fixed by $M_5$, $\alpha_5$ and
$\lambda$ throughout a run.

Define the scalar contractions
$\chi:=\boldsymbol m_5^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol S$,
$\xi:=\boldsymbol m_5^{\mathsf T}\mathcal D_x\boldsymbol S$, and
$\varrho:=\boldsymbol m_5^{\mathsf T}
(\boldsymbol S\times\mathcal D_x\boldsymbol S)$.
The twelve vector structures $\boldsymbol W_j$ used for the time
component are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol W_1&=\boldsymbol S\times\mathcal D_x\boldsymbol S,
&\boldsymbol W_2&=\varrho\boldsymbol m_5,\\
\boldsymbol W_3&=\boldsymbol S,
&\boldsymbol W_4&=\chi\boldsymbol m_5,\\
\boldsymbol W_5&=\mathcal D_x\boldsymbol S,
&\boldsymbol W_6&=\chi\mathcal D_x\boldsymbol S,\\
\boldsymbol W_7&=\xi\boldsymbol S,
&\boldsymbol W_8&=\boldsymbol m_5\times\mathcal D_x\boldsymbol S,\\
\boldsymbol W_9&=\chi(\boldsymbol m_5\times\boldsymbol S),
&\boldsymbol W_{10}&=\varrho\boldsymbol S,\\
\boldsymbol W_{11}&=\boldsymbol m_5,
&\boldsymbol W_{12}&=\chi^2\boldsymbol m_5.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ml-class5-features}
\end{equation}
The trainable coefficients are the spatial coefficient $\beta_5$ and
the twelve temporal coefficients $\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_{12}$.
The candidate vectors are
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol u_5
&=O_5\left(\frac{\ii}{2\lambda}\boldsymbol S
 +\beta_5\chi\boldsymbol m_5\right)
 -\frac{\alpha_5}{2}\boldsymbol m_5,
\label{eq:ml-class5-u}\\
\boldsymbol v_5
&=-2O_5\sum_{j=1}^{12}\theta_j\boldsymbol W_j.
\label{eq:ml-class5-v}
\end{align}
The coefficient of $\partial_t\boldsymbol S$ in
$\partial_t\boldsymbol u_5$ determines the matrix
\begin{equation}
\mathsf Q_5
:=O_5\left(\frac{\ii}{2\lambda}\Id_3
       +\beta_5\boldsymbol m_5\boldsymbol m_5^{\mathsf T}\right).
\label{eq:ml-class5-Q}
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathsf Q_5$ depends on the same $\beta_5$ as the spatial ansatz.
For a prescribed sparsity weight $\eta>0$ and smoothing scale
$\delta=10^{-10}$, the loss minimized over these thirteen coefficients is
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_5
={}&\frac{1}{12}\left\langle\left\|
O_5^{-1}
\bigl(\boldsymbol{\mathcal F}^{(5)}-\mathsf Q_5\boldsymbol E^{(5)}\bigr)
\right\|^2\right\rangle
\nonumber\\
&+\eta\left(\sqrt{\beta_5^2+\delta^2}
 +\sum_{j=1}^{12}\sqrt{\theta_j^2+\delta^2}\right).
\label{eq:ml-class5-loss}
\end{align}
The first term measures the curvature mismatch with a fixed, invertible
weighting $O_5^{-1}$ of its components.  The second term is a smooth
$\ell^1$ penalty applied uniformly to all thirteen coefficients.  The weight
$\eta$ is held fixed during each optimization stage, with the values
given in Section~\ref{sec:ml-numerics}.

The prescribed scalar parts are
\begin{align}
\mu_5&=\frac{1}{4\lambda}+\frac{\alpha_5}{4}S^+,
\nonumber\\
\nu_5&=\frac{\ii\alpha_5}{2}
 (S^+\partial_xS^3-S^3\partial_xS^+)
 +\frac{\ii\alpha_5^2}{4}(S^+)^2.
\label{eq:ml-class5-scalars}
\end{align}
They obey
$\partial_t\mu_5-\partial_x\nu_5=(\alpha_5/4)E^{(5)}_+$,
where $E^{(5)}_+$ is defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:class5-residual-plus}.
The scalar curvature therefore already has the required factorization.

The numerical coefficients approach the relations
\begin{align}
\beta_5&=-\frac{\ii\alpha_5^2\lambda}{4},
&\theta_1&=\frac{\ii}{2\lambda},
&\theta_2&=\beta_5,
\nonumber\\
\theta_3&=\frac{1}{4\lambda^2},
&\theta_4&=\frac{\alpha_5^2}{8},
&\theta_5&=\cdots=\theta_{12}=0.
\label{eq:ml-class5-coefficients}
\end{align}
Substitution gives exactly the matrices in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:class5-spatial-Lax} and
\eqref{eq:class5-time-Lax-compact}.  The identity
$\det\mathsf Q_5=(\ii/(2\lambda))^3$ and the inverse relations
\eqref{eq:class5-residuals-from-curvature} establish the local
equivalence to the equations of motion.

\subsection{Class 6}
\label{sec:ml-class6-paper}

For Class 6, the known nilpotency $N_6^3=0$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:class6-N-definition} determines the finite matrix basis
$\{\Id_3,N_6,N_6^2\}$.  The eight real coefficients
$a_1,a_2,b_0,b_1,b_2,c_0,c_1,c_2$ are the trainable parameters.
They enter three coefficient matrices,
\begin{align}
\mathsf A_6&=\frac{\ii}{2\lambda}\Id_3+a_1N_6+a_2N_6^2,
\nonumber\\
\mathsf B_6&=b_0\Id_3+b_1N_6+b_2N_6^2,
\nonumber\\
\mathsf C_6&=c_0\Id_3+c_1N_6+c_2N_6^2.
\label{eq:ml-class6-matrices}
\end{align}
The coefficient $\ii/(2\lambda)$ of $\Id_3$ in $\mathsf A_6$ is
fixed throughout a run.
The candidate vectors and scalar parts are
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol u_6&=\mathsf A_6\boldsymbol S,
&\mu_6&=\frac{1}{4\lambda},
\nonumber\\
\boldsymbol v_6&=-2\bigl[\mathsf B_6\boldsymbol S
 +\mathsf C_6(\boldsymbol S\times\partial_x\boldsymbol S)\bigr],
&\nu_6&=0.
\label{eq:ml-class6-ansatz}
\end{align}
Since $\partial_t\boldsymbol u_6=\mathsf A_6\partial_t\boldsymbol S$,
the spatial ansatz fixes the coefficient map multiplying
$\boldsymbol E^{(6)}$ to be $\mathsf A_6$.  The loss minimized over
the eight coefficients is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_6
=\frac{1}{12}\left\langle\left\|
\boldsymbol{\mathcal F}^{(6)}-\mathsf A_6\boldsymbol E^{(6)}
\right\|^2\right\rangle,
\label{eq:ml-class6-loss}
\end{equation}
evaluated on the independently sampled spin derivatives.  The nilpotency
of $N_6$ gives
$\det\mathsf A_6=(\ii/(2\lambda))^3$ for every choice of the fitted
coefficients, so its coefficient map is invertible for $\lambda\ne0$.

The recovered coefficient relations are
\begin{align}
a_1=c_1&=-\ii\alpha_6\lambda,
&a_2=c_2&=-\ii\alpha_6^2\lambda^3,
\nonumber\\
b_0&=\frac{1}{4\lambda^2},
&b_1&=\frac{\alpha_6}{2},
\nonumber\\
b_2&=\frac{3\alpha_6^2\lambda^2}{2},
&c_0&=\frac{\ii}{2\lambda}.
\label{eq:ml-class6-coefficients}
\end{align}
They give $\mathsf C_6=\mathsf A_6$ and the finite matrix-square-root
identity
\begin{equation}
\mathsf A_6^2=-\frac{1}{4\lambda^2}\Id_3+\alpha_6N_6.
\label{eq:ml-class6-square}
\end{equation}
The corresponding $U_6^{\rm fit},V_6^{\rm fit}$ coincide with
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:class6-spatial-Lax} and
\eqref{eq:class6-time-Lax-compact}.  Their subsequent analytic
verification gives
$\boldsymbol{\mathcal F}^{(6)}=\mathsf A_6\boldsymbol E^{(6)}$
off shell, with $\det\mathsf A_6=(\ii/(2\lambda))^3$.
Thus the final equivalence to the equations of motion follows from an
invertible coefficient map, as displayed explicitly in
Section~\ref{sec:class6-eom}.

\subsection{Sampling and numerical checks}
\label{sec:ml-numerics}

The following distributions generate the input data.  All dimensionful
quantities are expressed in a fixed length unit.
In Class 5, draw a real unit vector $\boldsymbol S$ by normalizing a
three-dimensional standard Gaussian vector.  Let
$\Pi_{\boldsymbol S}:=\Id_3-\boldsymbol S\boldsymbol S^{\mathsf T}$
be its tangent projection, and draw independent standard real Gaussian
vectors $\boldsymbol g_1,\boldsymbol g_2,\boldsymbol g_t$.  The local
derivatives are
\begin{align}
\partial_x\boldsymbol S
&=\Pi_{\boldsymbol S}\boldsymbol g_1-\frac{\alpha_5}{2}M_5\boldsymbol S,
\nonumber\\
\partial_x^2\boldsymbol S
&=\Pi_{\boldsymbol S}\boldsymbol g_2
 -(\Pi_{\boldsymbol S}\boldsymbol g_1)^{\mathsf T}
   (\Pi_{\boldsymbol S}\boldsymbol g_1)\boldsymbol S
\nonumber\\
&\quad-\alpha_5M_5\Pi_{\boldsymbol S}\boldsymbol g_1
 +\frac{\alpha_5^2}{4}M_5^2\boldsymbol S,
\nonumber\\
\partial_t\boldsymbol S&=-2\Pi_{\boldsymbol S}\boldsymbol g_t.
\label{eq:ml-class5-sampling}
\end{align}
These data satisfy the unit-spin constraint and its differentiated
identities; the time derivative is sampled independently of the
equations of motion.  Each run uses 768 training points and 4096
independent validation points.  The pairs $(\alpha_5,\lambda)$ are
$(0.8,5\ii/7)$, $(0.8,5\ii/12)$, and $(1.4,5\ii/12)$, with three
initializations for each pair.

For Class 6, draw a complex Gaussian vector $\boldsymbol z$, whose
real and imaginary components have independent unit variance, and set
$\boldsymbol S=\boldsymbol z/\sqrt{\boldsymbol z^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol z}$.
Samples with $|\boldsymbol z^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol z|\le0.25$ are rejected.
Using independent complex Gaussian vectors $\boldsymbol g_1,\boldsymbol g_2$
with the same distribution, set
\begin{align}
\partial_x\boldsymbol S&=0.8\Pi_{\boldsymbol S}\boldsymbol g_1,
\nonumber\\
\partial_x^2\boldsymbol S&=0.8\Pi_{\boldsymbol S}\boldsymbol g_2
 -(\partial_x\boldsymbol S)^{\mathsf T}(\partial_x\boldsymbol S)\boldsymbol S.
\label{eq:ml-class6-sampling}
\end{align}
For the off-shell loss, an independent complex Gaussian vector
$\boldsymbol g_t$ with the same distribution gives
$\partial_t\boldsymbol S=-2\Pi_{\boldsymbol S}\boldsymbol g_t$.
Each run uses 768 training points and 3072 independent validation points.
The pairs $(\alpha_6,\lambda)$ are $(0.35,10\ii/13)$,
$(0.7,\ii/2)$, and $(1.1,10\ii/29)$, with two initializations each.

Class 5 starts with independent zero-mean Gaussian coefficients of standard
deviation $0.25$.  Class 6 starts from the undeformed values
$b_0=1/(4\lambda^2)$, $c_0=\ii/(2\lambda)$, and zero for the remaining
coefficients, with independent zero-mean real Gaussian perturbations of
standard deviation $0.08$.
Both searches use Adam followed by the limited-memory
Broyden--Fletcher--Goldfarb--Shanno method (L-BFGS).
For Class 5, Adam has 600 steps and learning rate $0.03$;
L-BFGS has at most 50 iterations and learning rate $0.8$.
The sparsity weight in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ml-class5-loss} is
$\eta=10^{-9}$ during Adam and $5\times10^{-11}$ during L-BFGS.
For Class 6, Adam has 1300 steps and learning rate $0.02$;
L-BFGS has at most 120 iterations and learning rate $0.7$.
At every step, the loss is evaluated on the full, fixed training set,
and its gradients update the ansatz coefficients.  Both searches use
double precision.  After optimization, the same residuals are evaluated
on independently generated validation samples; the sparsity penalty is
excluded from the Class 5 validation residual.

Across the runs specified above, the largest absolute
coefficient error was $3.61\times10^{-7}$ in Class 5 and
$1.12\times10^{-11}$ in Class 6.  The largest absolute coefficient of
$\boldsymbol W_5,\ldots,\boldsymbol W_{12}$ was $8.10\times10^{-8}$.
The maximum validation mean-square residual in the first term of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ml-class5-loss} was $4.94\times10^{-14}$.
For Class 6, the maximum validation root-mean-square residual,
$\sqrt{\mathcal L_6}$, was $2.92\times10^{-11}$.
The analytic coefficient relations were used only for evaluation after
optimization.  Their exact substitution into the Lax matrices and the
off-shell identities in Sections~\ref{sec:class5-eom} and
\ref{sec:class6-eom} establish the physical results.  The evaluation of
overlapping quantum products in the main text supplies the further
connection to the finite lattice time operators.

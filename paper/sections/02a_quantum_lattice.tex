\subsection{Quantum spin chains and the lattice Lax equation}
\label{sec:quantum-lattice-setup}

The two lattice models studied in this paper are homogeneous periodic spin chains with
nearest-neighbour interactions and a two-dimensional local Hilbert space.  We
use the standard quantum inverse-scattering framework for such chains
\cite{Faddeev1996,AvanDoikouSfetsos2010}, with the following notation.

Let $\mathcal H_{\rm loc}\simeq\mathbb C^2$ denote a reference local Hilbert
space.  Consider a chain with $N$ lattice sites, and let $\mathcal H_n$ be a
copy of $\mathcal H_{\rm loc}$ assigned to the $n$th site,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_n \simeq \mathcal H_{\rm loc} \simeq \mathbb C^2,
\qquad n=1,\ldots,N.
\label{eq:local-hilbert-space}
\end{equation}
The Hilbert space of the full chain is the tensor product
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H^{(N)}
 = \mathcal H_1\otimes\mathcal H_2\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal H_N.
\label{eq:full-hilbert-space}
\end{equation}
We impose periodic boundary conditions, so the site labels are understood
modulo $N$; in particular, the site following $N$ is site $1$.

A homogeneous nearest-neighbour Hamiltonian is specified by a single
\emph{two-site Hamiltonian operator}
\begin{equation}
h\in \operatorname{End}(\mathcal H_{\rm loc}\otimes\mathcal H_{\rm loc}).
\label{eq:two-site-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
The adjective ``homogeneous'' means that the same two-site operator $h$ is
used on every nearest-neighbour bond.  We denote by $h_{n,n+1}$ the operator
on the full Hilbert space \eqref{eq:full-hilbert-space} that acts as $h$ on
\(\mathcal H_n\otimes\mathcal H_{n+1}\) and as the identity on all remaining
sites.  For $1\leq n<N$, this embedding is explicitly
\begin{equation}
h_{n,n+1}
 = \Id_{\mathcal H_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes
   \Id_{\mathcal H_{n-1}}\otimes h\otimes
   \Id_{\mathcal H_{n+2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes
   \Id_{\mathcal H_N}.
\label{eq:embedded-two-site-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
For the bond joining the last and first sites, $h_{N,1}$ denotes the same
operator $h$ acting on \(\mathcal H_N\otimes\mathcal H_1\), with the other
sites left unchanged.  Thus $h_{n,n+1}$ is the copy of the fixed two-site
operator $h$ on the $n$th bond.

With these conventions, the Hamiltonian of the periodic chain is
\begin{equation}
H^{\mathrm{lat}}
 = \sum_{n=1}^{N} h_{n,n+1},
\qquad h_{N,N+1}\equiv h_{N,1}.
\label{eq:generic-lattice-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
The model dependence therefore enters through the choice of the single
matrix $h$.  We next introduce the quantum $R$-matrix and the lattice Lax
equation associated with a nearest-neighbour integrable chain.


For the integrable chains considered in this paper, the local interaction is
encoded by a quantum $R$-matrix.  Introduce an auxiliary space
\begin{equation}
\mathcal V_a\simeq\mathcal H_{\rm loc}\simeq\mathbb C^2,
\label{eq:auxiliary-space}
\end{equation}
which is a tensor factor distinct from the physical Hilbert spaces
$\mathcal H_n$.  When several auxiliary spaces are required, we label them by
$a,b,c$.

Let $u\in\mathbb C$ denote the \emph{quantum spectral parameter}.  A quantum
$R$-matrix in the representation used here is a family of two-site operators
\begin{equation}
R(u)\in\End(\mathcal H_{\rm loc}\otimes\mathcal H_{\rm loc}).
\label{eq:quantum-R-matrix-definition}
\end{equation}
Whenever two tensor factors are individually labelled, the corresponding
subscripts indicate the copy of this same two-site operator acting on those
factors.  Thus, for example,
$R_{ab}(u)\in\End(\mathcal V_a\otimes\mathcal V_b)$ and
$R_{a,n}(u)\in\End(\mathcal V_a\otimes\mathcal H_n)$.

The models considered here have an $R$-matrix of difference form.  Its
Yang--Baxter equation is
\begin{align}
&R_{ab}(u-v)\,R_{ac}(u-w)\,R_{bc}(v-w)
\nonumber\\
&\hspace{30mm}=
R_{bc}(v-w)\,R_{ac}(u-w)\,R_{ab}(u-v),
\label{eq:quantum-yang-baxter}
\end{align}
which is an operator identity on
$\mathcal V_a\otimes\mathcal V_b\otimes\mathcal V_c$
\cite{Faddeev1996}.  The subscripts indicate the two tensor factors on which
each copy of $R$ acts.

Let
\begin{equation}
P\in\End(\mathcal H_{\rm loc}\otimes\mathcal H_{\rm loc})
\label{eq:local-permutation-operator}
\end{equation}
be the permutation operator defined by
$P(|v\rangle\otimes|w\rangle)=|w\rangle\otimes|v\rangle$.
As for the $R$-matrix, $P_{ab}$ denotes its copy on
$\mathcal V_a\otimes\mathcal V_b$.  We normalize the $R$-matrix so that its
regular point is $u=0$,
\begin{equation}
R(0)=P,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
R_{ab}(0)=P_{ab}.
\label{eq:R-regularity}
\end{equation}
For a regular fundamental spin-$\tfrac12$ chain, the two-site Hamiltonian
operator introduced in \eqref{eq:two-site-hamiltonian} is obtained from the
same two-site $R$-matrix as
\begin{equation}
h
=
P\left.\frac{\partial R(u)}{\partial u}\right|_{u=0}.
\label{eq:h-from-R}
\end{equation}
This is the standard relation between a regular $R$-matrix and the
nearest-neighbour Hamiltonian density in the quantum inverse-scattering
construction \cite{Faddeev1996,deLeeuwPribytokRyan2019}.

A scalar rescaling $R(u)\mapsto f(u)R(u)$ with $f(0)=1$ preserves the
regularity convention \eqref{eq:R-regularity} and adds
$f'(0)\Id$ to $h$.  This term shifts the total energy by a constant and leaves
the Heisenberg equations unchanged.  An overall rescaling of the spectral
parameter amounts to a corresponding normalization of the Hamiltonian.
These normalization freedoms are fixed together with each explicit
$R$-matrix.

To associate the $R$-matrix with a particular physical site, one copy of its
second tensor factor is identified with the local Hilbert space
$\mathcal H_n$.  The resulting \emph{quantum lattice Lax operator} is
\begin{equation}
L_{a,n}(u):=R_{a,n}(u)
\in\End(\mathcal V_a\otimes\mathcal H_n).
\label{eq:lattice-Lax-operator}
\end{equation}
For a fixed physical site $n$, the Yang--Baxter equation implies the local
exchange relation
\begin{align}
R_{ab}(u-v)L_{a,n}(u)L_{b,n}(v)
=
L_{b,n}(v)L_{a,n}(u)R_{ab}(u-v),
\label{eq:RLL-relation}
\end{align}
acting on $\mathcal V_a\otimes\mathcal V_b\otimes\mathcal H_n$.

The ordered product of lattice Lax operators around the periodic chain defines
the \emph{quantum monodromy matrix}
\begin{equation}
T_a(u)
:=L_{a,N}(u)L_{a,N-1}(u)\cdots L_{a,1}(u),
\label{eq:quantum-monodromy}
\end{equation}
and tracing over the auxiliary space gives the \emph{transfer matrix}
\begin{equation}
\tau(u):=\tr_a T_a(u).
\label{eq:quantum-transfer-matrix}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:RLL-relation} implies
\begin{equation}
[\tau(u),\tau(v)]=0,
\label{eq:commuting-transfer-matrices}
\end{equation}
so an expansion of $\tau(u)$ generates mutually commuting quantum charges
\cite{Faddeev1996}.
At the regular point, the logarithmic derivative of the transfer matrix
reproduces the nearest-neighbour Hamiltonian
\eqref{eq:generic-lattice-hamiltonian}, up to the scalar and normalization
freedoms described after \eqref{eq:h-from-R}.

We next need the local equation governing the time evolution of
$L_{a,n}(u)$.  Differentiating the Yang--Baxter relation at its regular point
and using \eqref{eq:h-from-R} gives the \emph{Sutherland relation}
\cite{deLeeuwPribytokRyan2019}
\begin{align}
&[h_{n-1,n},L_{a,n}(u)L_{a,n-1}(u)]
\nonumber\\
&\qquad=
L_{a,n}(u)\,\partial_u L_{a,n-1}(u)
-
\bigl(\partial_uL_{a,n}(u)\bigr)L_{a,n-1}(u).
\label{eq:Sutherland-relation}
\end{align}
Here $h_{n-1,n}$ denotes the embedding, defined above, of the same two-site
Hamiltonian $h$ on the neighbouring physical sites $n-1$ and $n$.

Let $t_{\rm lat}$ denote the quantum lattice time.  For an operator
$\mathcal O$ with no explicit time dependence we use the Heisenberg equation
\begin{equation}
\partial_{t_{\rm lat}}\mathcal O
=\ii[H^{\rm lat},\mathcal O].
\label{eq:Heisenberg-lattice-time}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:Heisenberg-lattice-time} defines the algebraic time
evolution used throughout the lattice Lax calculation.  For a non-Hermitian
Hamiltonian, normalized expectation values of right Schr\"odinger states in
the standard inner product define a separate evolution problem.  The
continuum comparison below concerns the algebraic evolution in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Heisenberg-lattice-time} and its coherent-state lower symbols.
Because $L_{a,n}$ acts only on the physical site $n$, only the two Hamiltonian
terms $h_{n-1,n}$ and $h_{n,n+1}$ contribute to its commutator with
$H^{\rm lat}$.  The Sutherland relation organizes these two contributions
into a discrete zero-curvature equation.

For spectral parameters at which $L_{a,n}(u)$ is invertible, define the
\emph{finite-lattice time Lax operator}
\begin{align}
A_{a,n}(u)
={}&\ii h_{n-1,n}
-\ii L_{a,n}(u)^{-1}h_{n-1,n}L_{a,n}(u)
\nonumber\\
&-\ii L_{a,n}(u)^{-1}\partial_uL_{a,n}(u).
\label{eq:finite-lattice-time-Lax}
\end{align}
The physical part of $A_{a,n}$ acts on the neighbouring sites
$n-1$ and $n$, while its matrix indices belong to the auxiliary space
$\mathcal V_a$.  Equations~\eqref{eq:Sutherland-relation} and
\eqref{eq:Heisenberg-lattice-time} then give
\begin{equation}
\partial_{t_{\rm lat}}L_{a,n}(u)
=
A_{a,n+1}(u)L_{a,n}(u)
-
L_{a,n}(u)A_{a,n}(u).
\label{eq:discrete-zero-curvature}
\end{equation}
This is the lattice analogue of a zero-curvature equation.  The time Lax
operator has a scalar ambiguity: adding to every $A_{a,n}$ the same
scalar function of $u$ times the identity on the full tensor product leaves
\eqref{eq:discrete-zero-curvature} unchanged.  This freedom permits convenient
normalizations.

The continuum limit of \eqref{eq:discrete-zero-curvature} depends on the
physical support of the operators in its two products.  They contain
operators that share a physical lattice site: $A_{a,n+1}$ and
$L_{a,n}$ both act on $\mathcal H_n$, while $L_{a,n}$ and
$A_{a,n}$ also overlap on $\mathcal H_n$.  Consequently, coherent-state expectation values of these products retain
correlations associated with the shared physical site.

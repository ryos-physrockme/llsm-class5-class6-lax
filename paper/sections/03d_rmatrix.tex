\subsection{Comparison with the classical \texorpdfstring{$r$}{r}-matrix construction}
\label{sec:class5-rmatrix-comparison}

The time Lax matrix in Eq.~\eqref{eq:class5-time-Lax-compact} was obtained by
starting from the finite-lattice time operator.  We now construct the time
component independently from the classical $r$-matrix and monodromy of the
continuum model.

To keep the two roles of the spectral parameter separate, let
$\zeta\in\mathbb C$ denote the \emph{generating spectral parameter} used to
expand the transfer matrix, while $\lambda$ continues to denote the Lax
spectral parameter of the final pair $(U_5,V_5)$.  Define the rank-one
coherent-state projector
\begin{equation}
\rho(x,t)
:=
\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
1+S^3&S^-\\
S^+&1-S^3
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\rho^2=\rho,
\qquad
\tr\rho=1,
\label{eq:class5-rho-definition}
\end{equation}
and the deformation matrix in the auxiliary space
\begin{equation}
K^{\mathrm{cl}}_5(x,t)
:=
\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
S^+&-(1+S^3)\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:class5-Kaux-definition}
\end{equation}
These are precisely the lower symbols in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:class5-P-lower-symbol} and
\eqref{eq:class5-K-lower-symbol}.  The spatial Lax matrix therefore admits the
form
\begin{equation}
U_5(x,t;\lambda)
=
\frac{\rho(x,t)}{2\lambda}
+\alpha_5 K^{\mathrm{cl}}_5(x,t).
\label{eq:class5-U-rho-K}
\end{equation}

We suppress the common time argument in the following formulas and first
fix the classical $r$-matrix in the conventions of this paper.  Let
$P_{12}$ be the permutation operator on two copies of the two-dimensional
auxiliary space.  On either copy, define the projector $P_{\uparrow}$ and
the matrix unit $E_{12}$ by
\begin{equation}
P_{\uparrow}:=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
E_{12}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:class5-rmatrix-elementary-matrices}
\end{equation}
and the constant Jordanian tensor
\begin{equation}
C_{12}
:=
P_{\uparrow}\otimes E_{12}
-
E_{12}\otimes P_{\uparrow}.
\label{eq:class5-jordanian-tensor}
\end{equation}
A direct calculation with the spin Poisson bracket
\eqref{eq:class5-poisson-bracket} gives
\begin{equation}
r^{(5)}_{12}(\zeta,\lambda)
=
\frac{\ii}{2(\zeta-\lambda)}P_{12}
+\ii\alpha_5 C_{12},
\label{eq:class5-classical-rmatrix}
\end{equation}
for which
\begin{align}
\{U_{5,1}(x;\zeta),U_{5,2}(y;\lambda)\}_5
={}&
\bigl[
 r^{(5)}_{12}(\zeta,\lambda),
 U_{5,1}(x;\zeta)+U_{5,2}(y;\lambda)
\bigr]\delta(x-y).
\label{eq:class5-linear-rmatrix-algebra}
\end{align}
Here
$U_{5,1}:=U_5\otimes\Id_2$ and
$U_{5,2}:=\Id_2\otimes U_5$ act on the first and second auxiliary spaces,
respectively.  The rational part of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:class5-classical-rmatrix} is the usual isotropic
Landau--Lifshitz contribution, whereas the constant term contains the Class 5
deformation.

Ref.~\cite{deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026} writes a quadratic Poisson
algebra for the finite classical Lax matrix obtained from its Class 5 quantum
Lax operator.  Equation~\eqref{eq:class5-linear-rmatrix-algebra} concerns the
long-wavelength coefficient $U_5$ defined by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:class5-L-lower-expansion}.  We evaluate this bracket directly
from the continuum spin Poisson bracket
\eqref{eq:class5-poisson-bracket}.  The finite classical Lax matrix and the
local connection $U_5$ therefore refer to successive stages of the same
Class 5 construction, with the continuum normalization fixed in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:class5-deformation-scaling}--\eqref{eq:class5-normalized-L}.
The $r$-matrix in Eq.~\eqref{eq:class5-classical-rmatrix} is consistent with
the Class 5 classical $r$-matrix of
Ref.~\cite{deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026} after the same convention
changes.

Let $\mathcal P$ denote path ordering with larger spatial coordinates to the
left.  The classical monodromy matrix is
\begin{equation}
T(x,y;\zeta)
:=
\mathcal P\exp\!\left(
\int_y^x U_5(s;\zeta)\,ds
\right).
\label{eq:class5-classical-monodromy}
\end{equation}
The associated transfer matrix on the periodic interval of length $\ell$ is
\begin{equation}
\tau(\zeta):=\tr T(\ell,0;\zeta).
\label{eq:class5-classical-transfer}
\end{equation}
For a linear classical $r$-matrix algebra of the form
\eqref{eq:class5-linear-rmatrix-algebra}, the standard monodromy construction
\cite{SemenovTianShansky1983,DoikouKaraiskos2011}
gives the generating time matrix
\begin{equation}
\mathbb V_2(x;\zeta,\lambda)
=
\frac{1}{\tau(\zeta)}
\tr_1\!\left[
T_1(\ell,x;\zeta)
\,r^{(5)}_{12}(\zeta,\lambda)\,
T_1(x,0;\zeta)
\right],
\label{eq:class5-rmatrix-generating-V}
\end{equation}
where the trace is taken over the first auxiliary space.  For
$\tau(\zeta)\ne0$ and $\zeta\ne\lambda$, it satisfies
\begin{equation}
\{\log\tau(\zeta),U_{5,2}(x;\lambda)\}_5
=\partial_x\mathbb V_2
 +[\mathbb V_2,U_{5,2}(x;\lambda)].
\label{eq:class5-generating-hamiltonian-flow}
\end{equation}

The pole of $U_5(x;\zeta)$ at $\zeta=0$ has residue $\rho/2$, by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:class5-U-rho-K}, with eigenvalues $1/2$ and $0$.
To extract local charges, we use the formal small-$\zeta$ expansion
associated with the first eigenvalue.  In a sector where
$\operatorname{Re}(1/\zeta)>0$, the corresponding monodromy eigenvalue
has the dominant exponential factor $\exp(\ell/(2\zeta))$.  Thus
\begin{equation}
\frac{T(x,0;\zeta)T(\ell,x;\zeta)}{\tau(\zeta)}
\sim\Pi(x;\zeta),
\label{eq:class5-normalized-monodromy-asymptotics}
\end{equation}
where $\sim$ denotes the formal power-series expansion, with the
exponentially small contribution of the other eigenvalue omitted.
The normalized monodromy at finite $\zeta$ has trace one; its local
expansion is described by the rank-one projector $\Pi$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\Pi^2=\Pi,
\qquad
\tr\Pi=1,
\qquad
\partial_x\Pi=[U_5(x;\zeta),\Pi],
\label{eq:class5-local-projector-equations}
\end{equation}
with the branch
\begin{equation}
\Pi(x;\zeta)
=
\rho(x)+\zeta\Pi_1(x)+\mathcal O(\zeta^2).
\label{eq:class5-local-projector-expansion}
\end{equation}
The projector constraint at order $\zeta$ and the transport equation
at order $\zeta^0$ give
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\rho\Pi_1+\Pi_1\rho&=\Pi_1,\\
\partial_x\rho&=\frac12[\rho,\Pi_1]
 +\alpha_5[K^{\mathrm{cl}}_5,\rho].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:class5-Pi1-equations}
\end{equation}
These determine the coefficient uniquely as
\begin{equation}
\Pi_1
=
2[\rho,\partial_x\rho]
-2\alpha_5[\rho,[K^{\mathrm{cl}}_5,\rho]].
\label{eq:class5-Pi1-solution}
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:class5-local-projector-expansion} in the generating
matrix \eqref{eq:class5-rmatrix-generating-V}, the local series reads
\begin{equation}
\mathbb V_2(x;\zeta,\lambda)
=
\mathbb V^{(0)}(x;\lambda)
+\zeta\mathbb V^{(1)}(x;\lambda)
+\mathcal O(\zeta^2).
\label{eq:class5-generating-V-expansion}
\end{equation}
The coefficient of $\zeta$ is
\begin{align}
\mathbb V^{(1)}
={}&
-\frac{\ii}{2\lambda}
\left(
\Pi_1+\frac{\rho}{\lambda}
\right)
\nonumber\\
&+\ii\alpha_5\left[
\tr(\Pi_1P_{\uparrow})E_{12}
-\tr(\Pi_1E_{12})P_{\uparrow}
\right].
\label{eq:class5-generating-V-first-coefficient}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:class5-generating-hamiltonian-flow} places the generating
Hamiltonian in the first argument of the Poisson bracket.  Antisymmetry
of the bracket converts this to the evolution convention in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:class5-hamiltonian-flow-definition}.  The coefficient of
$\zeta$ in $\log\tau(\zeta)$ therefore has the time Lax matrix
\begin{equation}
V_{5,r}(x;\lambda):=-\mathbb V^{(1)}(x;\lambda).
\label{eq:class5-rmatrix-time-V-definition}
\end{equation}

Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:class5-Pi1-solution} into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:class5-generating-V-first-coefficient} and using only the unit
spin constraint gives
\begin{equation}
V_{5,r}(x;\lambda)-V_5(x;\lambda)
=
\frac{\ii}{4\lambda^2}\Id_2.
\label{eq:class5-rmatrix-vs-quantum-V}
\end{equation}
The matrix on the right-hand side is independent of the spin field and of
the spatial coordinate.  Its spatial derivative vanishes and it commutes
with $U_5$, so $V_{5,r}$ and $V_5$ generate the same zero-curvature equation.
This is the scalar freedom described after Eq.~\eqref{eq:class5-DeltaV}.
The comparison identifies the associated local charge as generating the
$H^{\rm cl}_5$ spin flow.  This identification concerns the Hamiltonian
vector field on the unit-spin constraint; additive constants, Casimirs,
and total derivatives with vanishing periodic integral leave it unchanged.

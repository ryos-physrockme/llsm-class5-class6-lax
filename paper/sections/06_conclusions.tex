\section{Summary and discussion}
\label{sec:summary-discussion}

We have derived the continuum time Lax matrices of the Class 5 and
Class 6 Landau--Lifshitz models directly from their quantum lattice
time operators.  Products on overlapping physical sites contribute
at the same order as the continuum time derivative.  Retaining this
contribution gives a local correction of the direct lower symbol and
recovers the continuum zero-curvature equation.  The off-shell
curvature identities and their inverses establish equivalence with
all components of the independently derived Hamiltonian equations.
The classical $r$-matrix construction for Class 5 reproduces the same
time matrix up to a field-independent scalar.

The comparison in Section~\ref{sec:comparison} expresses both
corrections through the difference between the lower symbol of the
squared spatial coefficient and the square of its lower symbol.
The second spatial coefficient is responsible for the
additional commutator in Class 6, which can survive on a spatially
uniform configuration.  The correction also remains present in the
undeformed XXX limit.  These observations determine which terms of
the quantum operator expansion enter the continuum time component
in the two chains.

The construction uses the fixed spin-$\tfrac12$ representation and
the normalizations and smooth-field scalings specified above.  Its
curvature identities hold locally on the complex unit-spin constraint
for nonzero continuum spectral parameter.  Periodic boundary
conditions enter the Hamiltonian variation and the removal of total
derivatives.  The monodromy comparison in
Section~\ref{sec:class5-rmatrix-comparison} also uses the periodic
interval and the classical Poisson algebra.

The relation to quantum dynamics is established for instantaneous
derivatives: the lower symbols of exact lattice commutators, evaluated
on a product of coherent states at a fixed time, have the stated
Hamiltonian vector fields as their continuum limits.  Approximating
quantum evolution over a finite time interval would require control
of the correlations generated between sites and of the accumulated
continuum error.  A further question concerns the range of lattice
models for which the correction can be expressed in the form
\eqref{eq:comparison-common-source}.  Answering it requires the
operator expansions and overlapping-product calculations for those
models, and would clarify the conditions under which the difference
of second moments determines the continuum time correction.

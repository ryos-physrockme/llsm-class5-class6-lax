\section{Summary and discussion}
\label{sec:summary-discussion}

We have constructed the continuum time Lax matrices for the Class 5 and
Class 6 Landau--Lifshitz models by taking their quantum lattice time
operators to the long-wavelength limit at fixed spin $\tfrac12$.  The local
lattice zero-curvature equation contains products whose factors share a
physical site, and the lower symbols of these products cannot in general be
replaced by products of the separate lower symbols.  Under the scaling used
here, the coincident order-$\epsilon^2$ contributions cancel, while their
first spatial Taylor coefficients and the coincident order-$\epsilon^3$
terms remain at the continuum time-evolution order.

For the spin-$\tfrac12$ operator expansion specified in
Section~\ref{sec:shared-site-common-form}, this surviving source is organized
by the second-moment difference
$\Xi=(X^2)^\downarrow-(X^\downarrow)^2$.  Retaining the corresponding order
of the Sutherland relation gives
$\mathcal C=2\ii(\partial_x\Xi+[\Xi,U])$, so the contribution is absorbed
into a local correction of the time matrix.  The Class 5 and Class 6 finite-
lattice calculations independently reproduce this common identity, and the
corrected zero-curvature equations are equivalent to the separately derived
Hamiltonian equations of motion through off-shell identities with explicit
inverses.

The two models realize the common correction differently.  In Class 5 the
source is a total spatial derivative.  In Class 6 its model-specific
representation through the second spatial coefficient displays an additional
commutator, which remains nonzero on a spatially uniform spin configuration.
The general derivation also shows that explicit second-order spatial terms
cancel after all intermediate operator products are retained; the Class 6
coefficient is therefore a convenient representation of the second moment,
not independent data required by the common formula.

The final classical pairs are related to known structures, providing
independent checks rather than new classical hierarchies.  For Class 5, the
time matrix agrees with a classical $r$-matrix and monodromy construction up
to a field-independent scalar matrix.  After a complex field redefinition,
the traceless Class 5 pair is also mapped by a local auxiliary-space gauge
transformation to the known null-like warped $SL(2)$ Landau--Lifshitz pair,
as shown in Section~\ref{sec:class5-known-null-pair}.  The transformation is
not generically periodic, so this local equivalence does not identify the
periodic monodromies or global boundary sectors.  For Class 6, the spatial
matrix, time matrix and spin equation agree with the known rational
eleven-vertex Landau--Lifshitz structure after the convention dictionary in
Section~\ref{sec:class6-eleven-vertex-comparison}.

These comparisons sharpen the role of the lattice calculation.  The result
is not the discovery of a new Class 5 or Class 6 Lax hierarchy; it is the
direct recovery of their continuum time components from the finite-lattice
quantum time equation, including the local operator-product terms that
survive its fixed-spin continuum limit.  The correction is already present
in the undeformed XXX limit: the direct time symbol alone has twice the
correct coefficient of one term in the standard isotropic time matrix.

The numerical searches in Appendix~\ref{sec:ml-search} recover coefficients
consistent with both analytically verified pairs at the tested parameter
values.  Their role is to identify coefficient patterns inside the stated
finite ans\"atze; the Lax claims themselves are established by the analytic
operator calculation and the off-shell curvature identities.

The results are local statements on the complex unit-spin constraint surface
in the fixed spin-$\tfrac12$ scaling.  Periodicity is used for the Hamiltonian
variation and the Class 5 monodromy comparison.  The relation to quantum
dynamics concerns instantaneous derivatives obtained by evaluating exact
lattice commutators on product coherent states.  Controlling quantum
evolution over a finite time interval would additionally require estimates
for correlations generated between sites and for the accumulated error of
the continuum expansion.

A direct extension is to higher lattice Hamiltonians.  Their time operators
act on more physical sites, so the overlapping products can involve higher
local operator moments.  Comparing the resulting continuum flows with the
boost-generated charges of Ref.~\cite{deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026} and
the eleven-vertex hierarchy of Ref.~\cite{LevinOlshanetskyZotov2014} would
test how far the local formula of Section~\ref{sec:shared-site-common-form}
extends.  Varying the local spin representation would separately clarify the
relation between the long-wavelength limit used here and a large-spin
classical limit.

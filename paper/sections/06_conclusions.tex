\section{Summary and discussion}
\label{sec:summary-discussion}

We have constructed the continuum time Lax matrices for the Class 5
and Class 6 Landau--Lifshitz models by taking the quantum lattice
time operators to the long-wavelength limit.  The lower symbols
of products on overlapping physical sites yield a finite
contribution to the continuum Lax equation.  Its absorption into a
local time correction gives zero-curvature equations equivalent to
the independently derived Hamiltonian equations of motion.  This
equivalence follows from off-shell identities with explicit
inverses.  For Class 5, the time matrix also agrees with an
independent classical $r$-matrix construction up to the addition
of a field-independent multiple of the identity matrix.
The numerical searches in Appendix~\ref{sec:ml-search} recover coefficients
consistent with both pairs at the tested parameter values and provide
coefficient patterns for the analytic verification.

The comparison of the two models identifies the operator terms
required by this limit.  The Class 5 contribution is a total
spatial derivative, while the second spatial coefficient in
Class 6 produces an additional commutator.  A spatially uniform
configuration isolates that commutator.  The correction remains
present in the undeformed XXX limit, showing that the effect also
arises from the operator algebra of the isotropic chain.  These
results specify how the local quantum products determine the
continuum time component and provide a direct connection between
the lattice and classical constructions discussed in
Refs.~\cite{AvanDoikouSfetsos2010,deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026}.

The resulting Lax identities hold locally on the complex unit-spin
constraint in the fixed spin-$\tfrac12$ scaling.  Periodicity is used
for the Hamiltonian variation and the monodromy comparison.
The relation to quantum dynamics concerns the instantaneous
derivatives obtained by evaluating exact lattice commutators on
product coherent states.  An approximation to quantum evolution
over a finite time interval would also require estimates for the
correlations generated between sites and for the accumulated error
of the continuum expansion.

A direct extension is to apply the same construction to higher
lattice Hamiltonians.  This would permit a comparison with the
boost-generated continuum charges of
Ref.~\cite{deLeeuwFontanellaNietoGarcia2026} and the eleven-vertex
hierarchy of Ref.~\cite{LevinOlshanetskyZotov2014}.  As the time
operators act on more physical sites, their overlapping products
can involve additional operator moments.  Determining which of
these moments survive would test the range of the common formula
\eqref{eq:comparison-common-source}.  Studying the dependence on
the local spin representation would further clarify how the
long-wavelength limit combines with the classical limit of the
spin algebra.

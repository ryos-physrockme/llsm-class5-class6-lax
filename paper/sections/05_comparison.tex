\section{Comparison of Class 5 and Class 6}
\label{sec:comparison}

Section~\ref{sec:shared-site-common-form} gives the shared-site correction in
a form that is common to the spin-$\tfrac12$ scaling of the two chains.  We
now compare how that expression is realized by their different lattice
operators and deformation scalings, and then evaluate the common isotropic
limit.

\subsection{Operator-product corrections in the coherent-state limit}
\label{sec:comparison-symbol-products}

Let $m\in\{5,6\}$ label the model and define
\begin{equation}
\Xi_m
:=\left[\bigl(L_m^{(1)}\bigr)^2\right]^\downarrow-U_m^2,
\qquad
U_m=\bigl(L_m^{(1)}\bigr)^\downarrow.
\label{eq:comparison-symbol-square-difference}
\end{equation}
All lower symbols are evaluated at the same spin
$\boldsymbol S(x,t)$.  The Class 5 and Class 6 calculations in
Sections~\ref{sec:class5-time-lax} and \ref{sec:class6-time-lax}
independently give the common source
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_m
=2\ii\bigl(\partial_x\Xi_m+[\Xi_m,U_m]\bigr),
\label{eq:comparison-common-source}
\end{equation}
in agreement with Eq.~\eqref{eq:common-source-general}.  The representatives
used in the two model sections are obtained from
\begin{equation}
\Delta V_m
=2\ii\Xi_m-\frac{\ii}{4\lambda^2}\Id_{\mathcal V_a},
\qquad
V_m=V_m^\downarrow+\Delta V_m.
\label{eq:comparison-common-correction}
\end{equation}
The scalar term fixes the chosen representative and does not affect the
curvature.

The power counting behind Eq.~\eqref{eq:comparison-common-source} is the same
in both models.  The two connected products in the lattice zero-curvature
equation start at order $\epsilon^2$.  Their coincident-spin terms cancel,
while their first spatial Taylor coefficients and their coincident
order-$\epsilon^3$ terms survive at the same order as
$\partial_{t_{\rm lat}}\widehat L$ under
$t=\epsilon^2t_{\rm lat}$.  This is the local time-equation effect described
in Section~\ref{sec:shared-site-common-form}; it does not rely on treating the
nonmultiplicativity of coherent-state symbols as a new property.

\subsection{Common structure and model-dependent terms}
\label{sec:comparison-model-dependence}

The different deformation scalings follow from the bond lower symbols.  For
Class 5, the deformation vanishes at coincident spins and its leading term is
proportional to $\kappa_5\epsilon$.  For Class 6, it already contributes at
order $\kappa_6$.  Matching each contribution to the order-$\epsilon^2$
exchange energy fixes $\kappa_5=\epsilon\alpha_5$ and
$\kappa_6=\epsilon^2\alpha_6$.

The resulting Hamiltonian densities and spatial-operator coefficients are
compared in Table~\ref{tab:class5-class6-comparison}.  In the table,
$L_5^{(2)}:=0$ denotes the absent second-order coefficient in the exact
expansion \eqref{eq:class5-L-expansion}.  The matrices $M_5$ and $N_6$ are
those in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:class5-M-definition} and
\eqref{eq:class6-N-definition}.

The antisymmetric matrix $M_5$ couples the spin to its spatial derivative in
Class 5.  The symmetric matrix $N_6$ gives a local anisotropy term in Class 6.
At the operator level, the model-specific representations of the second
moment are
\begin{equation}
\Xi_5=\frac{\Id_{\mathcal V_a}}{4\lambda^2}-U_5^2,
\qquad
\Xi_6=\frac{\Id_{\mathcal V_a}}{4\lambda^2}
+2\bigl(L_6^{(2)}\bigr)^\downarrow-U_6^2.
\label{eq:comparison-two-symbol-squares}
\end{equation}
Thus $[\Xi_5,U_5]=0$, and the Class 5 source is a total spatial derivative.
For Class 6, Eq.~\eqref{eq:class6-L-first-square} expresses the same second
moment through $L_6^{(2)}$, making the nonzero commutator contribution
explicit in Eq.~\eqref{eq:class6-source-expanded}.  The spatially uniform
example \eqref{eq:class6-uniform-source} isolates this contribution: it
remains nonzero when all spatial derivatives vanish.

The appearance of $L_6^{(2)}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:comparison-two-symbol-squares}
does not mean that the common correction requires an independent
second-order spatial coefficient.  In the general derivation of
Section~\ref{sec:shared-site-common-form}, all explicit $Y$ terms cancel only
after they have been retained in the intermediate operator products, and
$\Xi$ can be reconstructed from the spin dependence of the leading matrix
through Eq.~\eqref{eq:common-Xi-from-U}.  The Class 6 identity
Eq.~\eqref{eq:class6-L-first-square} is a particularly convenient
model-specific representation of this quantity.

The undeformed limit also retains an operator-product correction.  To display
it, write $\sigma_a^i$ for the Pauli matrices acting on the auxiliary space
$\mathcal V_a$.  Setting $\alpha_m=0$ in either model gives
\begin{align}
\left.U_m\right|_{\alpha_m=0}
&=\frac{\Id_{\mathcal V_a}+S^i\sigma_a^i}{4\lambda},
\label{eq:comparison-isotropic-U}\\
\left.\Delta V_m\right|_{\alpha_m=0}
&=-\frac{\ii S^i\sigma_a^i}{4\lambda^2},
\qquad
\left.\mathcal C_m\right|_{\alpha_m=0}
=-\frac{\ii(\partial_x S^i)\sigma_a^i}{4\lambda^2}.
\label{eq:comparison-isotropic-correction}
\end{align}
These formulas follow from $(S^i\sigma_a^i)^2=\Id_{\mathcal V_a}$ on the
unit-spin constraint.  Adding the correction to the direct time symbol gives
the common isotropic Landau--Lifshitz time matrix
\begin{equation}
\left.V_m\right|_{\alpha_m=0}
=-\frac{(\boldsymbol S\times\partial_x\boldsymbol S)^i\sigma_a^i}
{2\lambda}
+\frac{\ii S^i\sigma_a^i}{4\lambda^2}.
\label{eq:comparison-isotropic-V}
\end{equation}
The direct symbol has twice the coefficient of the second term in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:comparison-isotropic-V}.  The surviving correction is therefore
already present in the spin-$\tfrac12$ XXX limit.

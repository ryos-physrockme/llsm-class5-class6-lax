\section{Comparison of Class 5 and Class 6}
\label{sec:comparison}

The two finite-lattice calculations allow us to identify the common
form of the surviving operator-product correction and the coefficients
that depend on the model.  We first express both corrections through
the difference between the lower symbol of an operator square and the
square of its lower symbol.  We then compare the deformation scalings
and spatial expansions, and evaluate the common isotropic limit.

\subsection{Operator-product corrections in the coherent-state limit}
\label{sec:comparison-symbol-products}

The lower-symbol correction in both models originates in the algebra of
operators acting on a common physical site.  For the Pauli matrices at
that site, let $\delta_{ij}$ denote the Kronecker delta and retain the
Levi-Civita convention $\varepsilon_{123}=1$.  The identity
$\sigma^i\sigma^j=\delta_{ij}\Id_2+\ii\varepsilon_{ijk}\sigma^k$
and the definition $S^i=(\sigma^i)^\downarrow$ give
\begin{equation}
(\sigma^i\sigma^j)^\downarrow
-(\sigma^i)^\downarrow(\sigma^j)^\downarrow
=\delta_{ij}-S^iS^j+\ii\varepsilon_{ijk}S^k.
\label{eq:comparison-single-site-product}
\end{equation}
The spin representation remains fixed at spin $\tfrac12$ as
$\epsilon\to0$, so this difference remains finite.  Smoothness controls
the variation of coherent-state labels between neighbouring sites.  The
operator products on their common site still obey
Eq.~\eqref{eq:comparison-single-site-product}.

In the discrete zero-curvature equation, the two overlapping products
have connected contributions starting at order $\epsilon^2$.  Their
leading terms cancel at coincident spins.  The first spatial Taylor
coefficients and the order-$\epsilon^3$ operator products then contribute
at the same order as $\partial_{t_{\rm lat}}\widehat L$ under
$t=\epsilon^2t_{\rm lat}$.  Sections~\ref{sec:class5-time-lax} and
\ref{sec:class6-time-lax} evaluate this surviving contribution explicitly.

For a common expression of the result, let $m\in\{5,6\}$ label the model
and define the auxiliary-space matrix
\begin{equation}
\Xi_m
:=\left[\bigl(L_m^{(1)}\bigr)^2\right]^\downarrow-U_m^2,
\qquad
U_m=\bigl(L_m^{(1)}\bigr)^\downarrow.
\label{eq:comparison-symbol-square-difference}
\end{equation}
All lower symbols here are evaluated at the same spin
$\boldsymbol S(x,t)$.  This definition extends the notation $\Xi_6$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:class6-symbol-square-difference} to Class 5.  For the two
normalized chains studied here, the finite-lattice calculations give
\begin{align}
\mathcal C_m
&=2\ii\bigl(\partial_x\Xi_m+[\Xi_m,U_m]\bigr),
\label{eq:comparison-common-source}\\
\Delta V_m
&=2\ii\Xi_m-\frac{\ii}{4\lambda^2}\Id_{\mathcal V_a},
\qquad
V_m=V_m^\downarrow+\Delta V_m.
\label{eq:comparison-common-correction}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:comparison-common-source} summarizes
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:class5-shared-site-correction} and
\eqref{eq:class6-shared-site-correction}.  Substituting
Eq.~\eqref{eq:comparison-common-correction} into it gives
$\mathcal C_m=\partial_x\Delta V_m+[\Delta V_m,U_m]$;
the field-independent scalar leaves this identity unchanged and fixes
the representatives used in the two model sections.

\subsection{Common structure and model-dependent terms}
\label{sec:comparison-model-dependence}

The different deformation scalings follow from the bond lower symbols.
For Class 5, the deformation vanishes at coincident spins and its leading
term is proportional to $\kappa_5\epsilon$.  For Class 6, it already
contributes at order $\kappa_6$.  Matching each contribution to the
order-$\epsilon^2$ exchange energy fixes
$\kappa_5=\epsilon\alpha_5$ and $\kappa_6=\epsilon^2\alpha_6$.
The resulting Hamiltonian densities and spatial-operator coefficients
are compared in Table~\ref{tab:class5-class6-comparison}.  In the table,
$L_5^{(2)}:=0$ denotes the absent second-order coefficient in the exact
expansion \eqref{eq:class5-L-expansion}.  The matrices $M_5$ and $N_6$ are
those in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:class5-M-definition} and
\eqref{eq:class6-N-definition}.

The antisymmetric matrix $M_5$ couples the spin to its spatial derivative
in Class 5.  The symmetric matrix $N_6$ gives a local anisotropy term in
Class 6.  At the operator level, the distinction relevant to the time
component is
\begin{equation}
\Xi_5=\frac{\Id_{\mathcal V_a}}{4\lambda^2}-U_5^2,
\qquad
\Xi_6=\frac{\Id_{\mathcal V_a}}{4\lambda^2}
+2\bigl(L_6^{(2)}\bigr)^\downarrow-U_6^2.
\label{eq:comparison-two-symbol-squares}
\end{equation}
Thus $[\Xi_5,U_5]=0$, and the Class 5 source is a total spatial
derivative.  For Class 6, the second spatial coefficient contributes
both a derivative and a commutator through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:class6-source-expanded}.  The spatially uniform example
\eqref{eq:class6-uniform-source} isolates the commutator contribution:
it remains nonzero even when all spatial derivatives vanish.

The undeformed limit also retains an operator-product correction.
To display it, write $\sigma_a^i$ for the Pauli matrices acting on the
auxiliary space $\mathcal V_a$.  Setting $\alpha_m=0$ in either model gives
\begin{align}
\left.U_m\right|_{\alpha_m=0}
&=\frac{\Id_{\mathcal V_a}+S^i\sigma_a^i}{4\lambda},
\label{eq:comparison-isotropic-U}\\
\left.\Delta V_m\right|_{\alpha_m=0}
&=-\frac{\ii S^i\sigma_a^i}{4\lambda^2},
\qquad
\left.\mathcal C_m\right|_{\alpha_m=0}
=-\frac{\ii(\partial_x S^i)\sigma_a^i}{4\lambda^2}.
\label{eq:comparison-isotropic-correction}
\end{align}
These formulas follow from $(S^i\sigma_a^i)^2=\Id_{\mathcal V_a}$ on the
unit-spin constraint.  Adding the correction to the direct time symbol
gives the common isotropic Landau--Lifshitz time matrix
\begin{equation}
\left.V_m\right|_{\alpha_m=0}
=-\frac{(\boldsymbol S\times\partial_x\boldsymbol S)^i\sigma_a^i}
{2\lambda}
+\frac{\ii S^i\sigma_a^i}{4\lambda^2}.
\label{eq:comparison-isotropic-V}
\end{equation}
The direct symbol has twice the coefficient of the second term in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:comparison-isotropic-V}.  The surviving correction is
therefore already present in the spin-$\tfrac12$ XXX limit.
